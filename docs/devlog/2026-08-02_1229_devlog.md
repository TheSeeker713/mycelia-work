# Devlog — Phase 0: Scaffold & Repo

**2026-08-02, 12:29**
**Author: Jeremy Robards, CTO & CAIO, Mycelia Interactive LLC**

Today I started building Mycelia Time. This is the app I've wanted for a while now, something that actually catches what I'm working on and when, without me having to fight it to get an honest log at the end of the day. I've tried the usual time trackers before and they always feel like they were built for someone billing a client by the hour, not for someone who just wants to remember what they actually did.

Before writing a line of code I had Claude do real research on this instead of just guessing at features. We looked into the psychology behind why people abandon time tracking apps and journaling habits. The Zeigarnik effect kept coming up, the idea that an unfinished or unrecorded task nags at you mentally until it's written down somewhere you trust. That single idea shaped a lot of my thinking here. If capturing a task is even a little bit annoying, I won't do it, and then the whole system falls apart. So the plan leans hard on making task capture and clock in basically frictionless.

We also dug into something I didn't expect to find so directly relevant, which is time blindness and recall bias. There's a study showing people under report their actual work hours by close to a full day per week when asked to recall them after the fact. That's not people lying, that's just how memory for time works, especially for me. So when I forget to clock out, which will happen, the app is not going to ask me "how many hours did you work yesterday" like that's a fair question. It's going to ask me anchored, multiple choice questions instead, and it's going to mark that time as estimated instead of pretending it's precise. That distinction matters to me.

The other big piece of today was figuring out how this plugs into OpenClaw. I already have OpenClaw running locally with Grok 4.5 as the default and a few Ollama models as fallback, and I wanted this app to just use whatever I've already got configured instead of managing its own API keys. Turns out the CLI has exactly what I need for that. And I found out I've already got a full local TTS setup sitting in my AI-Setup folder from an earlier project, Chatterbox-Turbo and Qwen3-TTS, both tested and working. So the audio cues I wanted for this app do not need any new setup or downloads. I get to reuse work I already did.

I also made two real decisions today instead of letting Claude assume. I picked Tauri over Electron for the shell, mostly because I want this thing to be light on the machine and I already have the Rust toolchain installed. And I decided timers should support more than one task running at once, since realistically I do context switch during a day and I don't want the app pretending I don't.

Then I asked for more once I saw the first draft of the plan. I want the OpenClaw side to wake up only when it's actually needed and let itself go quiet otherwise, since it's shared with other things I run on this machine. I want a real resource watchdog so this app can never be the thing that drags my system down. I want a proper flow for when I genuinely forget to clock out entirely, including the times I'm off doing physical work outside and nowhere near the computer. And I asked for a welcome sound and a goodbye sound too, small thing, but it matters to how the app feels to open and close.

What actually got built today is just the foundation. Tauri plus React plus TypeScript, Tailwind for styling, Vitest and Testing Library for tests, ESLint and Prettier wired in. Nothing exciting to look at yet, just a window that says "Mycelia Time" and confirms the scaffold runs. I ran the type checker, the linter, the test suite, and both the frontend and Rust builds before calling this phase done, and I actually read the output instead of trusting a green checkmark. All of it passed clean.

The repo is public now at github.com/TheSeeker713/mycelia-work, on main, like I wanted it from the start. Ten phases ahead of me: data layer, task dashboard, the multi timer core, todos and alerts, the actual AI integration, the resource watchdog, voice cues and the UI polish, export and backup, and finally packaging it into something that just runs on Windows 11 without me needing a dev environment installed.

Feels good to have this actually started instead of just talked about. Onto the data layer next.

— Jeremy
